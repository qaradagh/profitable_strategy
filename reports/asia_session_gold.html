Gold earns its drift in one hour of the day.

01

The clock is the whole finding

Mean return of each hourly bar on gold, by New York clock time. One bar is not like the others. The market is shut between 17:00 and 18:00 — that hour is the daily rollover, and the bar that opens immediately after it is the trade.

Gold return by hour of the New York day
Mean bar return in basis points · H1 data, 2023–2026 · 939 sessions
The 18:00 bar returns +4.73 bps; the next-largest hour manages 1.50, and the day's average outside it is near zero. On 5-minute data, +4.0 bps of that arrives in the first five minutes.
02

It held every single year

The effect was measured over the whole sample, so the honest test is whether any one period is carrying it. Splitting 13.6 years of H4 data into the session bar and everything else: the session bar is positive in all fourteen calendar years, including 2022, when gold itself went nowhere.

Session bar versus the rest of the day
Mean bar return in basis points, by calendar year · H4 data, 2013–2026
Session bar — 18:00 NY All other bars
Pooled: session bar +5.93 bps (t = 9.22, n = 3,505) against −0.34 bps (t = −1.09, n = 17,544) for every other bar. The same test on US500, US100, US30 and the four FX pairs returns t-statistics between −1.4 and +1.1 — nothing.

Why it should exist. This is a flow effect, not a chart pattern. Asian hours are when physical gold demand prints: the Shanghai Gold Exchange opens, and Asian refiners, jewellers and central banks buy in size against a dealer book that London and COMEX ran flat into the US close. Dealers short from the US afternoon cover into that demand. Its mirror image — gold drifting lower into the London PM fix — is a long-documented feature of the metal. A flow anchored to a market's opening hours does not decay the way a technical pattern does.

05

Choosing your risk level

Leverage scales the returns and the drawdowns together; it does not change the Sharpe. This is the honest trade-off, and it is where a return target has to meet reality.

LeveragePer monthCAGRVolatility Max drawdownWorst month

On a 5%-a-month target. That is 5.16% per month at 10× leverage, and the same history that produces it also produces a −58% drawdown. At 1% risk per trade with a sane stop, this setup returns about 0.55% a month. Getting to 5% is not a matter of a better entry — it is 10× the position size, and the drawdown scales with it. A Sharpe of 1.45 is a good number; it simply cannot produce 60% a year without 41% volatility.

3× is the level worth defending: 1.50% a month, −21.7% worst case.

06

The stop must be wide

This is the least intuitive rule in the setup and the easiest one to get wrong. The edge is a 6 bps drift riding on a bar whose standard deviation is 38 bps. A tight stop is not risk control here — it is a noise filter pointed the wrong way, and it converts a positive expectancy into a negative one.

07

What would break this

Stated plainly, because these decide whether it works for you rather than in a backtest.

1 · Your broker's spread at 18:00 New York

The single biggest dependency. The entry lands exactly at the daily rollover, when gold spreads are at their widest. The book breaks even at roughly 2× the assumed cost.

Sharpe against transaction cost
Cost multiple applied to spread, slippage and commission on every symbol

Because cost is a fraction of price, the breakeven spread rises as gold does: $0.48 when gold was $1,200, $2.42 at today's $4,620. The backtest's mean price was $1,837, so its cost drag is about 2.5× more punitive than what you would pay now.

Do this before risking money. Log your broker's actual XAUUSD spread at 18:00 New York every day for two weeks. Under $1.00 and the setup is comfortable. Over $2.50 and it is not tradeable at your broker — that is a fact about the broker, not about the edge.

2 · The edge is in the first minutes

On 5-minute data the first five minutes carry +4.0 of the hour's +5.1 bps. Entering ten minutes late loses most of it. This needs an order resting at the open — it is not a setup you can trade by hand at leisure.

3 · Financing is not optional

Long index CFDs pay roughly benchmark + 2.5% minus dividends. Charging it properly moves US500 buy-and-hold from 6.8% to 3.6% a year, and gold from 9.0% to 3.5%. The three-hour holding period is precisely why this setup survives it — and why buy-and-hold on a CFD does not.

4 · The three-hour exit rests on 3.6 years

H1 data only begins in 2023. The exit timing is confirmed over that window (Sharpe 2.53) and the concept over 13.6 years on H4 (Sharpe 1.34), but the specific three- versus-four-hour choice comes from the shorter sample. Both sit on a plateau, not a peak, so the choice between them is not load-bearing.

5 · Sleeves B and C are regime-dependent

Index mean reversion was negative in-sample 2017–2022 and positive 2023–2026; its parameter surface is mostly noise (in/out-of-sample rank correlation 0.23). It is capped at 7.5% for that reason. To simplify, trade the gold sleeve alone — it is 95% of the result.

08

How this was checked

A backtest that has not been attacked is a hypothesis, not a result. These are the attacks it survived.

  • ✓
    No block carries it. Split into 3-year blocks, every block is positive on its own — Sharpe 1.92, 1.08, 1.23, 0.64, 1.66.
  • ✓
    Look-ahead audit. Deliberately shifting the signal to trade the wrong bar collapses Sharpe from +1.35 to −1.27, confirming the return comes from the session bar and not from an off-by-one in the code.
  • ✓
    No filter fitting. Trend, prior-day direction, prior-day range, volatility regime and weekday were all tested; every one left the edge intact in both branches. Nothing was selected by search — the entry is deliberately unconditional.
  • ✓
    Monte Carlo. 5,000 block-bootstrap paths at 5× over three years: median +118%, 5th percentile +19%, median max drawdown −19%, probability of losing money 1.9%.
  • ✓
    Costs are pessimistic. Full spread every trade, extra slippage on top, entry spread doubled in the risk-sized test, and financing charged on actual calendar days including triple-charged weekends.
  • ✓
    Cross-asset placebo. The identical test on three indices and four FX pairs finds nothing (t between −1.4 and +1.1). A method that finds an effect everywhere is finding noise.
